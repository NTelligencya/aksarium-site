About Aksarium

Aksarium is a working archive of notes kept while learning several languages at once; Arabic, Russian, Bahasa Indonesia, Bengali and Persian, alongside a longer-standing interest in how writing systems are made, reformed, argued over, and read by machines.

It exists because a lot of the useful material sits between languages, and studying one language at a time throws it away. A root that surfaces in both Persian and Arabic with the sense quietly shifted; a colour boundary that Russian marks and English does not; a script reform that was never really about legibility. None of that belongs to a single language page, so the site is arranged to keep those connections.

Three sections hold the material. Codex Lexpressia is the script and keyboard reference; how writing systems work, how they are typed, transcribed and read by software, and the politics attached to all three. Notes from Somantasia is the written pieces; decipherment, mistranslation, and the claims people make about language and thought. The Morphorian Underground is the language learning resources; the vocabulary lists and the grammar lessons.

Nothing here is invented. Claims are sourced, figures are checkable, and where the evidence is thin, which in this field is often, it says so rather than rounding up. A good deal of what circulates about language is confidently wrong, and correcting that is part of the point.

The name

From Sanskrit akṣara (अक्षर), literally imperishable, from a- "not" plus kṣar- "to melt away"; it means a syllable, a letter. The same word came to mean a writing system in Javanese, Balinese and Old Malay. With -arium, a place where those are kept.
