Module 01

One app, two countries

Myth we'll unpick: "It's the same app with an English button."

Weixin (微信) and WeChat are one product, owned by Tencent, split in 2013 by a single mechanism: the phone number you register with. A mainland Chinese number creates a Weixin account under Chinese data and content law; any other number creates a WeChat account under the international terms. Same login screen, two sets of rules.

Outside the wall is not outside the system

In 2020, Citizen Lab tested what happens to documents and images shared entirely between international WeChat accounts; no mainland account anywhere in the conversation. The content was still put under surveillance, and the results were used to train the censorship system applied to China-registered users. An account outside the wall is not beyond the machine; it is one of the machine's inputs.

Citizen Lab, "We Chat, They Watch", Research Report No. 127, May 2020 (Knockel, Parsons, Ruan, Xiong, Crandall and Deibert). The lab's director, Ron Deibert, described it as "essentially undertaking political surveillance on one segment of users". Their earlier report, "One App, Two Systems" (2016), gave this module its name.

Why this matters

If you travel, your account type was decided the moment you typed your number; it is not a setting you can change later. Your wallet buys but cannot send. Your posts are moderated lightly but read closely. None of this is visible in the interface, which is precisely why it is worth fifteen minutes of your attention now rather than a confusing first week on the ground.

Standard Australian government travel advice for China (Smartraveller) separately recommends assuming no privacy on hotel or public Wi-Fi and taking care with social media in tense periods; ordinary published precautions, worth reading before departure.

Module 02 · A contrast case, not a travel guide

The walled garden made physical

Myth we'll unpick: "It's just a stricter version of the same internet."

North Korea's Kwangmyong (광명) is not a filtered internet. It is a separate network that was never connected to the world to begin with; the wall is not software, it is the topology itself. Module 01 showed control built into an app; this shows control built into the wires. You cannot visit Kwangmyong from outside the country, which makes how we know anything about it part of the lesson.

The operating system that watches its own files

At the 2015 Chaos Communication Congress, German researchers Florian Grunow and Niklaus Schiess presented the first deep analysis of Red Star OS, North Korea's home-built operating system. It is styled to look like macOS but is a modified Linux underneath; it stamps an identifying watermark into files, including those passed hand to hand on USB sticks, so the spread of unauthorised media can be traced; and it protects itself, with an integrity system that can reboot the machine if its core files are altered.

Grunow and Schiess, Chaos Communication Congress, Hamburg, 2015. Researcher Will Scott, who taught in Pyongyang and has handled the system in country, offers a counterpoint worth carrying alongside: the watermarking should be read in the context of a strictly internal, offline system, not as outward-facing spyware. The latest confirmed version is Red Star 4.0, which has never publicly leaked.

That is the terminal. Now the network it connects to.

What grows inside

Everything on this list is reconstructed from defector accounts, leaked builds, and the small number of foreigners who have used the network in country. There is no live source a learner could browse; treat the names as documented sightings, not a directory.

Naenara내나라 · "my country"The portal and its browser: a modified Firefox built by the state's Korea Computer Center, with forced automatic updates that researchers flag as a plausible monitoring channel.
Manbang만방 · IPTVA state streaming service; on-demand television over the intranet, one of the network's most visible consumer faces.
Domestic e-commerce상점 · shopping sitesOnline shopping grew inside the wall too; roughly twenty-two shopping sites were counted by 2021, per specialist reporting.
E-wallets삼흥 · 나래Competing payment apps, Samhung and Narae, covering buses, the metro, taxis and shops; balances shown in both won and a virtual "foreign-exchange won".
The mandatory app광명 앱Since 2022, smartphones must carry a state "Kwangmyong app" as a prerequisite for intranet access; it also enables location tracking and device monitoring. The garden's gate is on the phone itself.

Not a smaller internet; a different organism, grown for propagation and payment, not connection.

How we know any of this

Nothing in this module came from browsing Kwangmyong, because nobody outside the country can. It came from leaked operating-system builds, defector interviews, tour-operator photographs and satellite-watching specialists, cross-checked against each other. That is the real lesson of the contrast case: open-source research about a closed society is an assembled mosaic, and knowing how the tiles were sourced tells you exactly how far to trust the picture.
